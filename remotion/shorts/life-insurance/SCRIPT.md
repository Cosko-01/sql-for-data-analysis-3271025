# Short: "The Piggy Bank Inside Your Life Insurance"

## Facts (US rules; verify against the insurer's policy and a tax adviser before publishing)
- Term life insurance has no cash value. Only permanent policies (whole life, universal life) build a cash-value account.
- Part of each premium pays for the death benefit and costs; the rest goes to cash value, which grows tax-deferred.  (IRC §7702, defines life insurance for tax purposes)
- A policy loan is lent by the insurer, using the cash value as collateral. No credit check, no fixed repayment schedule. The cash value stays in the policy.
- The loan accrues interest (typically ~5-8% depending on insurer/policy). Unpaid interest is added to the loan balance.
- Any unpaid loan plus interest is subtracted from the death benefit.
- If loan + interest exceeds the cash value the policy can lapse; the loan above what you paid in premiums may become taxable income.  (IRC §72(e); MEC rules, §7702A)
- Cash value takes years to build; early on, little or nothing is available to borrow.

## Voice-over (~225 words, ~61 s at 3.7 words/s)
**Hook.** You can borrow money from your life insurance, and nobody checks your credit.

**Setting.** Meet Sam. Every month, Sam pays for a whole life policy. Part of that money buys protection. The rest drops into a savings pot called cash value.

**Action 1.** Years pass. The pot grows, and the taxman looks away.

**Action 2.** Then Sam wants a new oven for the bakery. The bank says no. The insurer says: that pot is yours. Borrow from it.

**Action 3.** The insurer lends its own cash, and keeps Sam's pot as collateral. No credit check. No due date.

**Snap.** But the loan charges interest. Five, six, seven percent. Sam never pays. The interest piles on top of the debt, and the debt keeps growing.

**Deadpan.** Luckily, nobody ever demands a payment. Unluckily, nobody ever demands a payment.

**Explanation.** Think of a pawn shop. Sam pawns the piggy bank, but keeps the piggy bank at home. Every year the shop adds interest to the ticket. If the ticket grows bigger than the piggy bank, the shop takes the whole thing, and the forgiven debt can become a tax bill.

**Consequence.** And if Sam dies with the loan unpaid, the insurer simply subtracts it from the family's payout.

**Callback twist.** So Sam's family got the insurance money, minus exactly one oven.

## Visual idea
Sam = baker bean-head. Pot = a giant glowing piggy bank in the town square. Loan = a rope from the piggy bank to the oven; interest = the rope growing a bigger knot each pass. Snap = the pig cracks. Twist = family opens the payout envelope, finds an oven-shaped hole.
