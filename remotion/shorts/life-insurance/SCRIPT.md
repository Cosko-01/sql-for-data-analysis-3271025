# Short: "How the Rich Borrow From Their Own Funeral"

## Facts (US rules; verify with the insurer's policy and a tax adviser before publishing)
- Permanent life insurance (whole/universal) builds a cash-value account that grows tax-deferred.  (IRC §7702)
- Policy loans are generally NOT taxable income while the policy stays in force and is not a Modified Endowment Contract (MEC).  (IRC §72(e), §7702A)
- Why the wealthy like it: borrowing instead of selling assets avoids realising capital gains. The loan has no credit check or repayment schedule; interest is added to the balance.
- Death benefit is generally income-tax free and repays the loan; the rest goes to heirs.  (IRC §101(a))
- Owned by an irrevocable life insurance trust (ILIT), the death benefit can stay outside the taxable estate (matters above the federal estate-tax exemption, ~$15M per person in 2026: verify).
- Premium financing: a bank lends the money for large premiums, the policy is collateral. Risk: rising rates or weak policy performance force extra collateral or a lapse.
- Private placement life insurance (PPLI) for ultra-wealthy: investments inside the policy; large minimums, accredited/qualified investors only.
- Risks: if loan + interest exceeds cash value the policy lapses and prior gains become taxable income at once. Fees, commissions and cost of insurance are high; early years have little cash value; designed for people who can fund premiums for decades.

## Voice-over (~230 words, ~62 s at 3.7 words/s)
**Hook.** Rich people borrow millions, often pay no income tax on it, and one of their favorite tools is life insurance.

**Setting.** Meet Mr. Gold. He pours huge premiums into a permanent life policy. Inside it, a pot of cash value grows, and the taxman looks away.

**Action 1.** When Mr. Gold needs cash, he does not sell his shares, because selling means a tax bill. He borrows from the policy instead.

**Action 2.** A policy loan is not income, so no tax. No credit check. No payment schedule. The insurer just adds interest to the balance.

**Action 3.** Some go even bigger. They borrow from a bank to pay the premiums, and the policy itself is the collateral. Debt, funding more shelter.

**Snap.** But it only works while the policy stays alive. Let the loan outgrow the pot, and the policy collapses, and years of hidden gains hit as taxable income, all at once.

**Deadpan.** Luckily, Mr. Gold has advisers. Unluckily, the advisers get commissions.

**Explanation.** Think of a pawn shop that never taxes you. Mr. Gold pawns his own piggy bank, again and again, and never sells a thing. When he dies, the payout clears the debt, tax-free, and the rest goes to his heirs. Put the policy in a trust, and it can sit outside his taxable estate.

**Consequence.** It is legal, but it was built for people who can feed premiums for decades. Fees are heavy, and early on there is almost nothing to borrow.

**Callback twist.** Mr. Gold never sold a single share. His taxman is still waiting.

## Visual idea
Mr. Gold = top-hat bean-head with a giant glowing piggy bank (the policy) in a vault in the town bank. Loan = a golden hose from the pig to his hand; interest = the hose slowly thickening. Premium financing = a second bank holding the pig's leash. Snap = pig cracks, old coins pour into a tax-collector's sack. Twist = the taxman at the door with a stopwatch, still waiting, cobwebs.
